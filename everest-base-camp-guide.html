Complete Guide to Everest Base Camp Trek 2026

Everything you need to plan the trek of a lifetime — the full 14-day itinerary, permit requirements, best seasons, gear list, realistic costs, and expert acclimatization tips from guides who've made this journey hundreds of times.

Share:

The Everest Base Camp Trek is not just a walk — it's a pilgrimage into the heart of the world's most dramatic mountain landscape. Every year, tens of thousands of trekkers from across the globe travel to Nepal to make this iconic journey through the Khumbu region, following the same trails used by legendary mountaineers including Sir Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay.

This guide, updated for 2026, draws on the experience of Beyond Summit's guides who have collectively completed this route over 400 times. Whether you're a seasoned trekker or setting foot on a Himalayan trail for the first time, here is everything you need to know.

What is the Everest Base Camp Trek?

Everest Base Camp (EBC) sits at 5,364 metres (17,598 feet) above sea level, at the foot of the Khumbu Icefall on the Nepalese side of Mount Everest. The classic EBC trek starts and ends in Lukla, reached by a short mountain flight from Kathmandu, and covers approximately 130 kilometres of trail through the Sagarmatha National Park — a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The route climbs through Sherpa villages, ancient Buddhist monasteries, rhododendron forests (in season), and the glaciated upper Khumbu, before arriving at the largest high-altitude base camp in the world. It's a journey through living Himalayan culture as much as it is a physical challenge.

Key Facts at a Glance

Distance: ~130km total (Lukla–Lukla) · Maximum altitude: 5,364m (EBC) / 5,550m (Kala Patthar) · Standard duration: 12–16 days · Best seasons: March–May and October–November · Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous · Technical climbing: None required

Best Time to Trek to Everest Base Camp

The Khumbu Valley is trekkable year-round, but two seasons dominate the EBC trekking calendar:

Spring (March to May)

Spring is the most popular trekking season, coinciding with the Everest climbing season. April and May offer the most stable weather, comfortable daytime temperatures (5–12°C at altitude), and spectacular views of the mountains before the monsoon haze arrives. The trails are busiest during this period — Namche Bazaar in May can feel surprisingly lively — but for a reason: the conditions are excellent.

Autumn (October to November)

Autumn is our personal favourite at Beyond Summit. Post-monsoon skies are crystal clear, offering exceptional photographic visibility. Temperatures are slightly cooler than spring, and trails are less crowded than peak spring. October offers the finest balance of weather and views.

Monsoon (June–September)

While technically passable, the monsoon season brings persistent cloud cover, leeches, flooded trails, and flight delays. Most experienced trekkers and operators avoid this window. December through February is cold — very cold above 4,000m — but offers dramatic winter solitude for experienced high-altitude trekkers.

14-Day Everest Base Camp Trek Itinerary

Below is Beyond Summit's standard 14-day EBC itinerary. We incorporate three acclimatization days rather than the two used by budget operators — a significant safety difference at this altitude.

Day1

Kathmandu → Lukla flight → Phakding

An early morning flight brings you to Lukla (2,860m) — one of the most thrilling runways in the world. A 3–4 hour walk along the Dudh Koshi River leads to Phakding (2,610m).

+0m altitude~8km walk
Day2

Phakding → Namche Bazaar

The biggest single ascent of the trek — 800 vertical metres through forest and over suspension bridges to reach the Sherpa capital, Namche Bazaar (3,440m). First views of Everest on a clear day.

+830m altitude~11km
Day3

Acclimatization Day — Namche Bazaar

A mandatory rest day at Namche. We recommend a day hike up to the Everest View Hotel (3,962m) for stunning panoramic views, returning to sleep at 3,440m. Visit Namche's Saturday market if timing aligns.

AcclimatizationOptional hike
Day4

Namche → Tengboche

Pass through Kyangjuma with breathtaking views of Ama Dablam, Everest, and Lhotse. Tengboche Monastery (3,867m) is the spiritual heart of the Khumbu — arrive in time for the evening puja ceremony.

+427m altitude~10km
Day5

Tengboche → Dingboche

Descend to Deboche, cross the Imja Khola, and climb through rhododendron forest to Pangboche before continuing to Dingboche (4,410m) — the gateway to the high Khumbu.

+543m altitude~12km
Day6

Acclimatization Day — Dingboche

Second acclimatization day. Hike to Nagarjun Hill (5,083m) for phenomenal views of the Island Peak, Makalu, and Lhotse South Face. This altitude day hike is excellent preparation for what lies ahead.

Acclimatization
Day7

Dingboche → Lobuche

Cross the terminal moraine of the Khumbu Glacier to Lobuche (4,940m). The landscape becomes increasingly austere and lunar. Pass the memorial chortens honouring lost mountaineers.

+530m altitude~8km
Day8

Lobuche → Gorak Shep → Everest Base Camp

The culminating day. Reach Gorak Shep (5,140m), drop your pack, and push on to Everest Base Camp (5,364m). Stand on the Khumbu Glacier and look up at the route taken by every Everest climber. Return to Gorak Shep.

+424m to EBC~16km round tripMost important day
Day9

Gorak Shep → Kala Patthar → Pheriche

Rise at 04:30 to summit Kala Patthar (5,550m) at sunrise — the highest point of the trek, with the definitive view of Everest's summit ridge. Descend all the way to Pheriche (4,243m).

Summit: 5,550mSunrise hike
Day10–14

Pheriche → Namche → Lukla → Kathmandu

Descend in four stages: Pheriche → Namche (Day 10), Namche → Lukla (Days 11–12), Lukla → Kathmandu flight (Day 13), Kathmandu buffer/rest day (Day 14). Descents feel dramatically faster than ascents.

Pro Tip: Acclimatization

Follow the golden rule: "Climb high, sleep low." Beyond Summit adds a third acclimatization day above 4,000m compared to budget operators. This dramatically reduces the risk of Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS). If you feel unwell at altitude — persistent headache, nausea, loss of coordination — descent is the only cure. Do not ascend with symptoms.

Permits Required for the EBC Trek (2026)

All trekkers need two permits to trek in the Khumbu region:

  1. Sagarmatha National Park Permit — NPR 3,000 (~USD 22) per person. Sagarmatha NP encompasses the entire Khumbu Valley including Everest.
  2. TIMS Card (Trekkers' Information Management System) — NPR 2,000 (~USD 15) for independent trekkers; covered by your agent if booking with a licensed operator like Beyond Summit.

When you trek with Beyond Summit Expeditions, all permits are arranged on your behalf before departure and included in the package cost.

Fitness & Training Requirements

The EBC trek requires no mountaineering skills, but it does demand a solid base of fitness. You will be walking for 5–8 hours per day at altitudes above 3,000 metres, often on uneven terrain. The cumulative elevation gain over the trek exceeds 8,000 metres.

We recommend a minimum of 8–12 weeks of dedicated preparation before your trek, including:

  • Weekly long hikes (3–6 hours) on hilly terrain, ideally with a loaded daypack
  • Cardiovascular training 3–4 times per week (running, cycling, swimming)
  • Stair climbs with a weighted pack to simulate ascent effort
  • Core and leg strengthening exercises (squats, lunges, step-ups)

If you are over 50 or have any pre-existing cardiovascular or respiratory conditions, consult your physician and consider a medical high-altitude consultation before booking.

Essential Gear Checklist

Packing correctly is half the battle. Your base weight should not exceed 10–12kg; porters (included in Beyond Summit packages) carry the bulk of your kit. Below are the essentials:

  • Footwear: Waterproof, ankle-supporting trekking boots (broken in before the trek), warm camp shoes/sandals, gaiters
  • Layering system: Moisture-wicking base layers, mid-layer fleece or softshell, insulated down jacket (800+ fill), waterproof hardshell jacket and trousers
  • Sleeping: Down sleeping bag rated to –15°C (most teahouse rooms lack adequate heating above 4,500m)
  • Accessories: Warm hat, balaclava, liner gloves + waterproof outer gloves, UV-protective sunglasses, SPF 50+ sunscreen
  • Trekking poles: Strongly recommended, especially for descent
  • Medications: Ibuprofen, diamox (consult GP), oral rehydration salts, blister kit, personal prescriptions
  • Electronics: Power bank (teahouses charge for electricity), camera, offline maps downloaded

Accommodation: What to Expect

Teahouses (local lodges) form the backbone of accommodation on the EBC route. Quality has improved dramatically over the past decade, but expectations should be calibrated to altitude. Below Namche, rooms are comfortable with attached bathrooms in most lodges. Above 4,500m, expect basic shared facilities and no guarantee of hot showers.

Beyond Summit selects lodges along the route based on hygiene standards, food quality, and heating. Our premium packages include twin-share rooms throughout; single rooms are available at a supplement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a guide for the EBC trek?

Since April 2023, solo, unguided trekking in Nepal's national parks is no longer legally permitted. All trekkers must be accompanied by a licensed guide. Beyond Summit's guides are TIMS-registered, fluent in English, and have completed wilderness first aid training.

Can I trek EBC without prior high-altitude experience?

Yes. The EBC trek reaches 5,364m, which is challenging but manageable without prior Himalayan experience if you are fit, follow your guide's pace, and take acclimatization seriously. We have guided trekkers in their 60s and 70s to Base Camp.

What's the failure rate on the EBC trek?

Industry statistics suggest 7–10% of trekkers do not reach Base Camp, primarily due to altitude sickness. Beyond Summit's itinerary — with three acclimatization days — gives our guests a significantly higher completion rate.
