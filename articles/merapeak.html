About this climb

At 6,476 m, Mera is the highest of Nepal's trekking peaks, yet technically one of the easiest: a long glacier walk in crampons to a summit with five 8,000 m peaks in view. We take the slower way in, through quiet villages east of Lukla, so your body has time to adjust.

  • Five 8,000 m peaks from one summit
  • A non-technical glacier climb, ideal as a first 6,000 m peak
  • The remote Hinku valley, with few other trekkers
  • Gradual approach via Pangkongma for better acclimatisation
  • Acclimatisation and glacier training day at Khare
  • Exit over the Zatrwa La back to Lukla

Permits and practical details

Permits you need

  • Mera Peak climbing permit (Nepal Mountaineering Association)
    Seasonal royalty, lowest in autumn
  • Makalu Barun National Park entry
    NPR 3,000
  • Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality fee
    Collected at Lukla

NMA royalties were revised in September 2025. We include the current fee for your season.

Altitude and safety

Above 3,000 m we check everyone's oxygen level and pulse each evening, and talk through how you're sleeping and eating. If altitude symptoms don't ease with rest, we go down. No summit or pass is worth pushing on for. Your insurance needs to cover helicopter rescue up to 6,476 m.

Where you stay

Tea houses on the walk in, then two-person tents at base camp and high camp. At base camp a cook team looks after you, with a dining tent and a toilet tent.

Fitness and preparation

You need good hill fitness, some experience walking in crampons and a head for heights. When you book, we send a training plan and a full kit list, and we check your gear together in Kathmandu.

Common questions

Is Mera Peak harder than Island Peak?

Technically Mera is easier: there is no steep headwall, only a glacier walk and a short steep section near the top. But it is 290 m higher, so altitude is the main challenge. Many people climb Mera first and Island Peak later.

Why not take the faster route over the Zatrwa La?

The Zatrwa La route reaches 4,600 m on the second day, too fast for many people. Our way in via Pangkongma is longer but much gentler; we use the Zatrwa La on the way out.

Will I see Everest from the summit?

On a clear day you see five of the world's six highest mountains: Everest, Kangchenjunga, Lhotse, Makalu and Cho Oyu.
