Complete Guide to Annapurna Base Camp Trek 2026

Standing inside the Annapurna Sanctuary — a natural amphitheatre of 8,000-metre peaks — is unlike anywhere else on earth. This guide covers the full 12-day itinerary, ACAP permit requirements, gear checklist, realistic costs, and everything a first-timer or returning trekker needs to know before setting foot on this legendary trail.

Share:

If the Everest Base Camp trek is Nepal's most famous trail, the Annapurna Base Camp trek is its most beloved. Where EBC offers raw high-altitude drama and a sense of mountaineering history, the ABC trek delivers something rarer: the feeling of being completely enclosed within a circle of Himalayan giants, the world falling away below you, the sky impossibly blue above.

The route passes through some of Nepal's most diverse terrain — terraced rice paddies, dense rhododendron forests, traditional Gurung villages, and glacial moraines — all within a single trek. It is shorter and lower in maximum altitude than EBC, making it more accessible to a broader range of trekkers. But do not mistake accessible for easy. The trail demands respect, preparation, and the right guide at your side.

Key Facts at a Glance

Distance: ~115km total (Nayapul–Nayapul) · Maximum altitude: 4,130m (ABC) · Standard duration: 10–14 days · Best seasons: March–May and October–November · Difficulty: Moderate · Technical climbing: None required · Starting point: Nayapul, 1.5 hrs from Pokhara

What is the Annapurna Base Camp Trek?

Annapurna Base Camp sits at 4,130 metres (13,549 feet) at the heart of the Annapurna Sanctuary — a high glacial basin ringed by some of the world's most dramatic peaks. The most prominent is Annapurna I (8,091m), the tenth-highest mountain on Earth and, famously, the first 8,000-metre peak ever climbed by humans, summited by Maurice Herzog's French expedition in 1950.

The surrounding amphitheatre includes Annapurna South (7,219m), Hiunchuli (6,441m), Gangapurna (7,455m), and the sacred, never-climbed Machhapuchhre — "Fishtail" (6,993m). The Sanctuary itself is a glaciated bowl accessible through a narrow gorge along the Modi Khola river, hemmed in by glaciers on both sides. The sense of arrival here is unlike anything on any other trek in Nepal.

Best Time to Trek to Annapurna Base Camp

The ABC trek can be completed year-round, but two windows offer the finest conditions.

Spring (March to May)

Spring is the most spectacular season for the ABC trek, and for one reason above all others: rhododendrons. From mid-March through April, the forest between Ghandruk and Chhomrong erupts in crimson, pink, and white blooms — Nepal's national flower at its most dramatic. Daytime temperatures at altitude are comfortable (8–14°C), mornings are clear, and afternoon clouds typically build but rarely cause serious disruption. April is our top recommendation for the ABC trek.

Autumn (October to November)

Post-monsoon skies are crystalline. The rhododendrons are gone but the visibility is extraordinary, offering some of the sharpest mountain photography of any season. Temperatures drop faster than spring — nights below Chhomrong can be genuinely cold by November — but the trails are slightly less crowded than peak spring. October strikes the ideal balance.

Monsoon (June–September) & Winter (December–February)

The Annapurna Sanctuary is particularly susceptible to avalanche during and immediately after heavy monsoon snowfall. The gorge leading into the Sanctuary can be blocked for days. While some experienced trekkers do attempt the route in winter for the solitude, the risk of trail closure and severe cold above 3,500m is significant. Budget extra days and carry comprehensive equipment if trekking outside the two main seasons.

12-Day Annapurna Base Camp Itinerary

This is Beyond Summit's standard 12-day ABC itinerary, departing from Pokhara. It incorporates a rest day at Chhomrong and a cultural half-day in Ghandruk — an approach that results in better acclimatisation, a higher completion rate, and a far richer overall experience than the cut-price 10-day versions.

Day1

Pokhara → Nayapul → Tikhedhunga

A 1.5-hour drive from Pokhara to the trailhead at Nayapul (1,070m). Begin walking through Birethanti village and along the Modi Khola valley to Tikhedhunga (1,480m). A gentle warm-up day through rhododendron forest and terraced farmland.

+410m altitude~12km walk
Day2

Tikhedhunga → Ghorepani

A demanding day of climbing, ascending more than 1,500 stone steps through jungle to Ulleri (1,960m), then continuing through dense rhododendron and oak forest to Ghorepani (2,860m). The effort is repaid with the first teaser views of the Annapurna range.

+1,380m altitude~13km
Day3

Ghorepani → Poon Hill Sunrise → Tadapani

Rise at 05:00 for the iconic Poon Hill (3,210m) sunrise — a 360° panorama of Dhaulagiri, the Annapurnas, Machhapuchhre, and beyond. Return to breakfast, then continue through thick rhododendron to Tadapani (2,630m).

Poon Hill: 3,210mSunrise highlight
Day4

Tadapani → Ghandruk

Descend to Ghandruk (1,940m), one of the largest and most culturally rich Gurung villages in Nepal. Explore the stone-paved village lanes, the Gurung Museum, and the monastery. This cultural half-day is deliberately unhurried — Ghandruk deserves proper time.

Cultural village day~6km
Day5

Ghandruk → Chhomrong

Cross the Modi Khola and climb steeply to Chhomrong (2,170m), the last major village before the Sanctuary and the gateway to the high trail. Excellent close-up views of Annapurna South and Hiunchuli begin here. Settle in for an acclimatisation day tomorrow.

+230m altitude~8km
Day6

Rest & Acclimatisation — Chhomrong

A well-earned rest day in Chhomrong. We recommend a morning hike up to the Chhomrong Monastery viewpoint (approx. 2,400m) for early morning mountain views, returning to lower elevation to sleep. Afternoon at leisure — explore the village, visit the hot springs at nearby Jhinu Danda if time allows.

AcclimatisationOptional Jhinu Danda hot springs
Day7

Chhomrong → Himalaya (Dovan)

Enter the Annapurna Sanctuary gorge in earnest. Descend to the Modi Khola at Sinuwa (2,360m), then pass through Bamboo (2,310m) and climb through increasingly alpine terrain to Himalaya Hotel (2,920m). Bamboo forest gives way to rhododendron and then open alpine scrub.

+750m net altitude~10km
Day8

Himalaya → Machhapuchhre Base Camp (MBC)

Pass through Deurali (3,230m) — an exposed saddle prone to afternoon weather — and cross the glacial terrain to Machhapuchhre Base Camp (3,700m). The sacred Fishtail peak towers directly above. Weather can deteriorate rapidly here; an early start is essential.

+780m altitude~8kmEarly start essential
Day9

MBC → Annapurna Base Camp → MBC

The defining day. A two-hour push from MBC brings you to Annapurna Base Camp (4,130m) — the Sanctuary. You are surrounded on all sides by 6,000–8,000-metre peaks. Annapurna I fills your view to the north. Allow ample time to absorb it. Return to MBC or descend to Himalaya for the night.

ABC: 4,130m summit point~6km round trip
Day10

Descent: Himalaya → Chhomrong

Descending the Sanctuary gorge is significantly faster than the ascent. Pass back through Bamboo and Sinuwa, returning to the warmth and comfort of Chhomrong. Celebrate with a well-earned hot shower and dinner.

Long descent day~14km
Day11

Chhomrong → Jhinu Danda → Nayapul → Pokhara

Descend to Jhinu Danda — where the natural hot springs offer a perfect post-trek soak — then continue down to Nayapul. Drive back to Pokhara by afternoon. Evening at leisure in Lakeside.

Hot springs at JhinuPokhara by afternoon
Day12

Buffer Day — Pokhara / Return to Kathmandu

A built-in buffer for flight or weather delays, recovery, or further exploration of Pokhara (Phewa Lake, the World Peace Pagoda, or a paragliding flight over the valley). Drive or fly back to Kathmandu as required.

Flexible rest dayPokhara / Kathmandu
Avalanche Awareness in the Sanctuary

The gorge between Deurali and MBC passes beneath steep slopes that can avalanche following heavy snowfall. Always follow your guide's advice on timing. Beyond Summit monitors weather conditions in the Sanctuary from Pokhara before and during each trek. If conditions require an early descent or an extra day in Chhomrong, that call will always be made without hesitation.

Permits Required for the ABC Trek (2026)

Two permits are required for the Annapurna Base Camp trek, and both must be carried throughout the trek as they are checked at multiple checkpoints:

  1. ACAP — Annapurna Conservation Area Project Permit — NPR 3,000 (~USD 22) per person. The entire trekking region lies within the Annapurna Conservation Area, managed by the National Trust for Nature Conservation. Your permit fees go directly towards conservation and local community projects.
  2. TIMS Card (Trekkers' Information Management System) — NPR 2,000 (~USD 15) for trekkers booking through a licensed agency. Trekker registration is mandatory for all trekkers in Nepal.

Fitness & Preparation

The ABC trek is graded moderate, making it one of the most accessible Himalayan trekking experiences. However, "moderate" still means 6–7 hours of walking per day on steep, often uneven terrain. The single toughest day — the climb from Tikhedhunga to Ghorepani — involves over 1,300 metres of ascent, much of it on stone staircases.

We recommend a minimum of 6–8 weeks of preparation, including:

  • Regular hikes of 3–5 hours with 500–800m of elevation gain, wearing your trekking boots
  • Cardiovascular training 3 times per week — running and cycling are ideal
  • Step or stair climbing with a loaded pack (10–12kg simulated daypack weight)
  • Yoga or stretching for flexibility and injury prevention, particularly knees and ankles

The ABC trek does not require prior high-altitude experience. Its maximum altitude of 4,130m is achievable with proper pacing, adequate hydration, and one quality acclimatisation day built into the itinerary.

Knee Protection on Descent

The descent from Chhomrong to Nayapul involves thousands of stone steps and is notoriously hard on knees. Trekking poles are not optional on the ABC trek — they are essential. Bring or rent a quality pair in Pokhara before departure, and take the descents slowly. Our guides apply strapping tape and carry knee braces for clients who need them.

Essential Gear Checklist

The ABC trek reaches a maximum of 4,130m — cold at night but not extreme by Himalayan standards. Your pack weight should stay under 8–10kg; porters carry the main duffel bag on Beyond Summit packages.

  • Footwear: Waterproof, ankle-supporting trekking boots (broken in), gaiters for monsoon or winter season, lightweight camp shoes
  • Clothing layers: Moisture-wicking base layers, mid-layer fleece, insulated jacket (down or synthetic), waterproof hardshell jacket and trousers
  • Sleeping: Down sleeping bag rated to –10°C (teahouses above 3,000m are poorly heated and blanket quality varies)
  • Accessories: Warm hat, buff/neck gaiter, liner gloves and waterproof outer gloves, UV sunglasses, SPF 50+ sunscreen
  • Trekking poles: Non-negotiable for the stone staircases on this route
  • First aid: Ibuprofen, altitude sickness medication (Diamox — consult your GP), oral rehydration salts, blister kit, insect repellent (lower elevations in spring)
  • Water: Two 1-litre bottles or a hydration bladder; water purification tablets or a filter (teahouses sell bottled water but minimising plastic is encouraged)
  • Electronics: Headtorch with spare batteries, power bank (electricity costs NPR 100–200 per charge above Chhomrong), camera
Gear Rental in Pokhara

Pokhara's Lakeside area has excellent gear rental shops. Sleeping bags, down jackets, trekking poles, and duffel bags are all readily available at reasonable daily rates. Beyond Summit's pre-departure briefing includes a gear check — if you are missing anything, we'll direct you to the most reliable rental shops in Lakeside.

Accommodation on the Trail

The Annapurna region has the most developed teahouse network in Nepal, shaped by decades of trekking tourism. Below Ghorepani and in Ghandruk and Chhomrong, you will find genuinely comfortable lodges with private rooms, hot showers, Western toilets, and excellent food — the dal bhat in Chhomrong is, without exaggeration, some of the best on any trekking route in Nepal.

As you enter the Sanctuary above Himalaya Hotel, facilities become simpler. Rooms are basic twin-share or dorm-style at MBC and ABC itself. Bring your sleeping bag regardless of the season — teahouse blankets above 3,500m are never adequate. Nights are cold; the shared dining room with a wood stove is where community forms among trekkers on the trail.

Beyond Summit selects teahouses based on hygiene, food quality, and reliability. Our repeat relationships with lodge owners mean our guests receive priority room allocation during the busy spring season, when MBC and ABC teahouses can fill completely.

Cultural Highlights: What Makes This Trek Unique

The ABC route passes through the heartland of the Gurung people — one of Nepal's most prominent hill communities, with a long tradition of military service in Gurkha regiments and a rich animist-Buddhist cultural heritage. Ghandruk is the finest Gurung village accessible to trekkers anywhere in Nepal. Walking its slate-paved lanes, with Annapurna South framed at the end of every alley, is a highlight in itself.

Above the villages, the Annapurna Conservation Area was established in 1986 as Nepal's first protected area managed by a local trust rather than the government — an internationally recognised conservation model. The permit fees you pay fund anti-poaching patrols, reforestation programs, and the maintenance of the stone staircase trails that have served these communities for centuries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a guide mandatory for the ABC trek?

Yes. Since April 2023, Nepal requires all trekkers in national parks and conservation areas to be accompanied by a licensed guide. Beyond Summit's guides are TIMS-registered, English-speaking, and trained in wilderness first aid. Attempting to trek without a guide risks permit confiscation and a fine.

Can I do the ABC trek without prior experience?

Absolutely. The ABC trek's maximum altitude of 4,130m and moderate difficulty rating make it an excellent first Himalayan trek for fit individuals. We have guided trekkers in their 70s to the Sanctuary. The key factors are fitness, appropriate pacing, and the right guide-to-client ratio.

How cold does it get at Annapurna Base Camp?

At 4,130m, overnight temperatures at ABC drop to between –5°C and –15°C depending on season, with wind chill amplifying the cold significantly. Daytime temperatures in the Sanctuary are usually 0–8°C in spring and autumn. A sleeping bag rated to –10°C or lower is essential above 3,500m.

What is the success rate for reaching ABC?

With a properly paced itinerary and no underlying health issues, the success rate on the ABC trek is very high — above 90% on Beyond Summit departures. The most common reason for turning back is weather closure of the gorge above Deurali, which is rare in peak season but possible. Our itinerary builds in flexibility to wait out a day's bad weather in Chhomrong without abandoning the summit goal.

Do I need altitude sickness medication (Diamox)?

Diamox (acetazolamide) is a prescription medication that helps speed acclimatisation. At ABC's altitude of 4,130m, most healthy trekkers do not need it if the ascent is properly paced. We recommend consulting your GP or a travel medicine clinic before your trip. Our guides carry oxygen and a pulse oximeter on every trek and monitor all clients from Chhomrong upward.
