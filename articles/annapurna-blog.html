About this trek

This trek takes you into the Annapurna Sanctuary, a high glacial basin ringed by Annapurna I, Annapurna South and the sharp summit of Machapuchare. We go in by way of Poon Hill and the Gurung villages of the foothills, so in under two weeks you get rhododendron forest, terraced hillsides and a night surrounded by snow peaks.

  • Sunrise over Dhaulagiri and Annapurna from Poon Hill
  • A night inside the Annapurna Sanctuary at 4,130 m
  • Machapuchare, the sacred unclimbed 'Fishtail' peak
  • Gurung villages of Ghorepani and Chhomrong
  • Natural hot springs at Jhinu Danda
  • No domestic flights: road access from Pokhara

Permits and practical details

Permits you need

  • Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)
    NPR 3,000 per person (foreign nationals)
  • TIMS card
    Required in the Annapurna region

Both are included in our price and arranged before you arrive in Pokhara.

Altitude and safety

Above 3,000 m we check everyone's oxygen level and pulse each evening, and talk through how you're sleeping and eating. If altitude symptoms don't ease with rest, we go down. No summit or pass is worth pushing on for. Your insurance needs to cover helicopter rescue up to 4,130 m.

Where you stay

Simple, family-run tea houses: twin rooms, shared bathrooms higher up, and a warm dining room around the stove where everyone gathers in the evening. Hot showers and phone charging cost a few dollars extra.

Fitness and preparation

If you can walk for six hours on hilly ground several days in a row, you'll be fine. Two or three months of regular hill walks with a loaded daypack is the best preparation.

Common questions

Is Annapurna Base Camp good for a first Himalayan trek?

Yes. The highest night is 4,130 m, much lower than Everest Base Camp, and you can reach the trailhead by road. The challenge is the steps: thousands of stone stairs up and down between villages.

Is there avalanche risk on the way to the sanctuary?

The section between Dovan and Machapuchare Base Camp lies under steep slopes. In late winter and early spring, after heavy snow, avalanches can cross the trail. Our guides check conditions each morning with lodge owners and the ACAP office, and we wait or turn back if the slope is loaded.

Do I need to fly?

No. Pokhara is a 25-minute flight or a seven-hour drive from Kathmandu, and the trek starts a short drive from Pokhara.

Can I add more days?

Yes. Many people add Mardi Himal or a few days in Pokhara. Tell us your dates and we'll suggest a plan.
