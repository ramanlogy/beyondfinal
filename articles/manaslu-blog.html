Complete Guide to Manaslu Circuit Trek 2026

The Manaslu Circuit is Nepal's great secret — a 177-kilometre circuit around the world's eighth-highest mountain through villages that see a fraction of the traffic of Everest or Annapurna. This guide covers everything you need to know: the restricted area permits, the dramatic Larkya La Pass crossing, the full 16-day itinerary, realistic costs, and why guides who've walked both routes consistently rate Manaslu as their favourite.

Share:

There is a moment on Day 3 of the Manaslu Circuit, somewhere between the village of Jagat and the emerald gorge below Deng, when it becomes clear that this is not the same Nepal that fills the Instagram feeds of trekking tourists. The villages here — built in stone, strung with prayer flags, governed by ancient rhythms — have changed little in centuries. The trail is narrower. The teahouses simpler. And Mount Manaslu (8,163m), the eighth-highest mountain on Earth, rises above it all with an authority that feels entirely unperformed.

The Manaslu Circuit Trek circumnavigates this mountain in its entirety — a 177-kilometre loop through subtropical river gorges, dense rhododendron and pine forest, high-altitude Tibetan Buddhist villages, and across a genuinely challenging glacial pass at 5,106 metres. It is one of the finest long-distance mountain treks anywhere on the planet. It is also a restricted area trek, which is precisely why it remains so extraordinary.

Restricted Area Trek — What This Means for You

The Manaslu region is a government-designated restricted area, meaning it requires a special permit (RAP) in addition to the standard MCAP and TIMS cards. Trekking alone is not permitted — you must be accompanied by a licensed guide and travel with at least one other person. Beyond Summit handles every aspect of the permit process. What is the Manaslu Circuit Trek?

The circuit follows the Budhi Gandaki River north from the lowland town of Arughat, climbing through one of Nepal's most dramatic river gorges before entering the high-altitude Nubri and Tsum regions — areas settled by Tibetan-origin communities that have maintained their language, religion, and architecture largely untouched by modernity.

The trek crosses the Larkya La Pass (5,106m) — a demanding high glacial pass that is the physical and psychological centrepiece of the circuit — before descending into the Marsyangdi Valley and finishing at Dharapani, where the Annapurna Circuit trail begins. Many trekkers combine the two, creating one of Nepal's great multi-week mountain journeys.

Manaslu itself was first summited in 1956 by a Japanese expedition led by Toshio Imanishi and Gyalzen Norbu. The mountain's name derives from the Sanskrit word manasa, meaning "mountain of the spirit." Walking around it for two weeks, that name feels entirely earned.

Comparing Manaslu Circuit with Everest Base Camp or Annapurna Circuit? Manaslu is longer, higher at the pass, and significantly more remote than both. It suits trekkers who have already completed one Himalayan trek and want something wilder, less crowded, and more culturally immersive. Best Time to Trek the Manaslu Circuit

Autumn (September to November) — Our Top Pick

Autumn is the finest season for Manaslu. The post-monsoon air is crystal clear, the rhododendrons and maples of the lower valley turn in October, and the high-altitude sections above Samagaon are at their most photogenic. October is the peak month — stable weather, exceptional visibility of the full Manaslu massif, and comfortable temperatures up to the pass (–5°C to –15°C at night on the Larkya La crossing). September is viable but can still carry residual monsoon cloud in the lower gorge.

Spring (March to May)

Spring is the second main trekking window. Rhododendrons bloom spectacularly in the lower valleys through March and April — the forest between Soti Khola and Namrung is among the finest rhododendron trekking in Nepal. Larkya La is generally in good condition by mid-April. Spring has slightly softer light than autumn but warmer days and a more vivid lower trail.

Monsoon & Winter Closures

The Budhi Gandaki gorge is subject to serious landslides during monsoon (June–September), making sections of the lower trail genuinely hazardous and periodically impassable. Larkya La can be buried under several metres of snow from December through February, with no teahouse support above Samdo. The Manaslu Circuit should not be attempted in monsoon or deep winter without extensive high-altitude experience and fully self-sufficient camping equipment.

16-Day Manaslu Circuit Itinerary

Beyond Summit's 16-day itinerary includes two dedicated acclimatisation days at Samagaon (3,530m) before the Larkya La crossing — a critical safety measure that budget operators routinely skip. The extra day here significantly reduces the risk of altitude sickness on the pass and on the final descent to Bimthang.

Day1

Kathmandu → Arughat by jeep/bus → Soti Khola

A full day of driving (7–9 hours) from Kathmandu takes you to Arughat (550m), the administrative centre of the Gorkha district and your last reliable ATM. Continue a short walk or drive to Soti Khola (730m) where the trail begins.

Long drive dayWithdraw cash in Arughat
Day2

Soti Khola → Machhakhola

The first day on trail follows the Budhi Gandaki River through subtropical forest, crossing suspension bridges and passing through small Gurung villages. The air is warm, the river is dramatic, and the pace is a gentle introduction to what lies ahead. Machhakhola (869m) is a simple but comfortable teahouse stop.

+139m altitude~18km walk
Day3

Machhakhola → Jagat

The gorge begins to tighten and deepen. Cross the river multiple times on wire suspension bridges as the trail climbs through bamboo and rhododendron. Jagat (1,340m) is the first permit checkpoint — your RAP, MCAP, and TIMS will be inspected here. A significant milestone: you are now in the restricted zone.

+471m altitudePermit checkpoint~16km
Day4

Jagat → Deng

One of the finest days on the trek: the gorge narrows to near-vertical walls as the Budhi Gandaki churns through a series of waterfalls and rapids. The trail cuts across cliff faces on narrow ledges, occasionally tunnelling through rock. Arrive at Deng (1,860m), where Tibetan Buddhist culture begins to assert itself — prayer walls, mani stones, and the first glimpses of Manaslu's ridge line.

+520m altitudeGorge narrows~14km
Day5

Deng → Namrung

The landscape shifts dramatically. Subtropical forest gives way to pine and juniper. Villages become more Tibetan in character — flat-roofed stone houses, chortens, and monks in burgundy robes. Namrung (2,630m) offers the first proper views of the Manaslu group and the satellite peaks of Ganesh Himal.

+770m altitudeCulture shift~12km
Day6

Namrung → Samagaon

Pass through the atmospheric villages of Lihi and Sho — where yak caravans still carry loads between the high pastures and the valley floor — before arriving at Samagaon (3,530m), the largest and most culturally rich village on the circuit. The Pungyen Monastery above the village is one of the most beautifully situated gompas in Nepal. Manaslu's south face dominates the skyline.

+900m altitude~12km
Day7

Acclimatisation Day — Samagaon

First acclimatisation day at Samagaon. The prescribed activity is a hike to the Manaslu Base Camp (4,480m) and back — a superb day hike with extraordinary close views of Manaslu's massive south face and the Thulagi Glacier. Return to sleep at Samagaon (3,530m). The puja ceremony at Pungyen Monastery in the evening is not to be missed.

AcclimatisationBC day hike: 4,480m
Day8

Samagaon → Samdo

A short but important stage — ascend to Samdo (3,865m), a high-altitude village just 10km from the Tibet border that functions as the final staging point before the Larkya La. The trail to Samdo passes glacial lakes and boulder fields. Spare time in the afternoon for a rest hike toward the Larkya Glacier to preview tomorrow's terrain.

+335m altitude~8kmTibet border area
Day9

Second Acclimatisation Day — Samdo

The second acclimatisation day at Samdo. Hike toward the Larkya Glacier moraine (approx. 4,300m) to further extend your altitude exposure without sleeping high. This day is the most important preparation investment on the entire trek — guides who skip this day are creating real risk for their clients on Larkya La the following morning.

AcclimatisationGlacier moraine hike
Day10

Samdo → Dharmasala (Larkya Phedi) → Larkya La Pass (5,106m) → Bimthang

The defining day. Depart at 04:00 from Dharmasala (4,480m) in darkness by headtorch. The pre-dawn crossing of the Larkya Glacier — crevasses marked by guide poles, the Manaslu massif silhouetted against the pre-dawn sky — is among the most cinematic experiences in Himalayan trekking. Reach the Larkya La Pass (5,106m) as the sun rises over the Annapurna range. A long, demanding descent on loose moraine brings you to the green sanctuary of Bimthang (3,590m). Total day: 10–12 hours of walking.

Day11

Bimthang — Rest and Recovery

An unhurried rest day at Bimthang (3,590m), a beautiful elevated meadow with superb views back toward the Larkya La and ahead toward the Annapurna peaks. Most trekkers sleep deeply, eat substantially, and feel the full relief of having crossed the pass. Waterfalls, yak herds, and rhododendron forest surround the settlement.

Recovery dayViews of Annapurnas
Day12

Bimthang → Tilje → Dharapani

Descend through pine and oak forest, passing the waterfall village of Tilje, to Dharapani (1,960m) — the point where the Manaslu Circuit meets the Annapurna Circuit trail. The descent into the Marsyangdi Valley is rapid and rewarding. Dharapani marks the official end of the restricted Manaslu zone.

–1,630m descentEnd of restricted zone
Day13–14

Dharapani → Besisahar → Kathmandu (Drive)

Drive down the Marsyangdi Valley from Dharapani to Besisahar (823m), then onward by bus or jeep to Kathmandu or Pokhara (7–9 hours). Day 14 serves as a built-in buffer for road conditions, delays, or an extra rest night in Besisahar or Pokhara.

Combining with the Annapurna Circuit

Finishing the Manaslu Circuit at Dharapani puts you at the start of the classic Annapurna Circuit. Many trekkers add 7–10 additional days to complete the Annapurna Circuit, crossing the Thorong La Pass (5,416m) and descending to Muktinath and Pokhara. This combined 24–26-day route is one of the great extended Himalayan treks and well within reach for anyone completing Manaslu. Ask our team about the combined package.

Restricted Area Permits — Everything You Need to Know

The Manaslu region's restricted area status is not bureaucratic inconvenience — it is the reason this trek exists in the form it does. Permit requirements limit daily trekker numbers, fund local conservation, and have prevented the over-commercialisation that has transformed parts of the Annapurna and Everest circuits.

Three permits are required, and all must be carried and presented at checkpoints throughout the route:

Fitness Requirements — Is This Trek Right for You?

The Manaslu Circuit is rated Strenuous. This is not a first-timer's route. Ideal candidates have completed at least one major Himalayan trek — the Annapurna Base Camp trek, the EBC trek, or an equivalent — and are confident at altitude. The Larkya La crossing at 5,106m, preceded by a 04:00 predawn start and up to 12 hours of continuous walking, demands genuine physical and mental resilience.

We recommend a minimum of 10–14 weeks of structured preparation, including:

  • Weekly long hikes of 5–8 hours on mountainous terrain, with a loaded pack of 10–12kg
  • Multiple overnight hikes to practise sustained multi-day effort
  • Running or cycling at least 3 times per week to build VO2max capacity
  • Stair-climbing and leg-press exercises for the major ascents and, critically, the long descents
  • Prior experience at altitude above 4,000m is strongly recommended before Larkya La
The Larkya La: What to Expect

The Larkya La Pass (5,106m) is a genuine high mountain pass, not a manicured viewpoint. The crossing involves 4–5 hours of ascent on glacier moraine and loose scree in pre-dawn darkness, followed by a rocky glacier traverse (no technical equipment required, but crampons are carried as emergency gear by Beyond Summit guides), and a 6–7 hour descent on steep, unstable terrain to Bimthang. Weather can deteriorate without warning. The pass is closed by heavy snowfall and is not negotiable in poor visibility. Our guides hold the authority to turn the group around at any point if conditions require it — this decision is never overruled.

Gear Checklist for the Manaslu Circuit

The Manaslu Circuit demands a more serious kit than either the EBC or ABC treks. The combination of sub-tropical lower gorge (hot and humid), high alpine upper circuit (cold and windy), and the Larkya La pass crossing (potentially extreme) means your layering system needs to cover a 40°C temperature range across the trek.

  • Footwear: Waterproof, ankle-supporting trekking boots with full Vibram sole (not trail runners — the descent from Larkya La on loose moraine demands rigid ankle support). Gaiters are essential for the pass crossing.
  • Layering: Moisture-wicking base layers (merino preferred), mid-layer fleece or softshell, insulated 800-fill down jacket, waterproof hardshell jacket and trousers rated to at least 10,000mm hydrostatic head
  • Pass crossing: Warm balaclava, liner gloves + expedition outer mitts, neck gaiter, UV-protective goggles (for glacier glare)
  • Sleeping: Down sleeping bag rated to –20°C — teahouses above Samdo are unheated stone shelters. Do not underestimate the cold at Dharmasala (Larkya Phedi) the night before the crossing.
  • Trekking poles: Absolutely mandatory — both for the gorge sections and for the descent from Larkya La
  • Medications: Diamox (consult GP), ibuprofen, oral rehydration salts, blister and wound kit, personal prescriptions
  • Navigation: Offline maps downloaded (Maps.me or Gaia GPS), printed route card — mobile signal is absent for large sections of the circuit
  • Electronics: High-capacity power bank (20,000mAh minimum), headtorch with fresh lithium batteries and spare set, satellite communicator recommended for independent emergencies
  • Cash: Withdraw sufficient Nepali rupees in Arughat or Kathmandu. There are no ATMs on the circuit above Arughat.
Gear Rental in Kathmandu

High-quality mountaineering gear for the Larkya La — rated sleeping bags, expedition down jackets, trekking poles, gaiters, and duffel bags — can be rented from reputable shops in Thamel. Beyond Summit's pre-departure briefing in Kathmandu includes a gear check and can connect you with trusted rental partners. Do not rely on finding suitable gear in Arughat or on the trail itself.

Culture Along the Route: The Nubri People

What separates the Manaslu Circuit from every other major Himalayan trek is the depth and authenticity of its cultural encounter. The upper Budhi Gandaki valley — the Nubri and Tsum regions — was settled by Tibetan refugees and Tibetan-origin communities over centuries, and it retains a living Buddhist culture that is increasingly rare anywhere in the Himalayan world.

In Samagaon, the morning puja at Pungyen Monastery begins before dawn, the low rumble of the conch shell and the smell of butter lamps drifting across the village. Yak herders in the high pastures above Samdo speak Tibetan as their first language. Children in Lihi and Sho study in village schools built by the proceeds of the very trekking permit system that funds your trek.

The restricted area status has protected these communities from the rapid teahouse commercialisation that transformed the Annapurna and Everest regions in the 1980s and 90s. Walking here, it feels like what all Himalayan trekking must once have been — an exchange between travellers and a living mountain civilisation, rather than a transaction with a tourism industry.

Accommodation: Setting Expectations

The Manaslu Circuit teahouse network is simpler and more variable than on the Everest or Annapurna routes. Below Jagat and through the lower gorge, lodges are comfortable with private rooms, decent food, and functional bathrooms. Above Namrung, facilities become progressively more basic. Above Samagaon, trekkers should expect shared dorm rooms, basic pit toilets, and limited hot water.

At Dharmasala (Larkya Phedi, 4,480m) — the last stop before the pass — accommodation is stone-walled dormitory sleeping with no heating, no showers, and limited food. Bring your sleeping bag, fill your thermos with hot water before sleep, and eat a proper meal at Samdo before the short evening walk up.

Beyond Summit's team books ahead on all stages, ensuring priority rooms and confirming food availability on the upper circuit where supplies can be limited during peak season. Our clients eat well — this is not incidental; proper nutrition is a safety consideration at altitude.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Manaslu Circuit suitable for first-time Himalayan trekkers?

In most cases, no — and we are honest about this. The combination of restricted area logistics, the remoteness of the upper circuit (evacuation from above Samagaon is difficult and expensive), and the physical demands of the Larkya La make this a trek best suited to those who have already completed a major Himalayan route. If this is your first Nepal trek, start with Annapurna Base Camp and return for Manaslu — the contrast between the two will make both more meaningful.

What happens if someone gets altitude sickness on the Manaslu Circuit?

Evacuation from the upper circuit is by helicopter, which is expensive and weather-dependent. All Beyond Summit guides carry a pulse oximeter, supplemental oxygen, and a portable altitude chamber (Gamow bag) on the upper sections. We monitor oxygen saturation daily above 3,500m. The single most important thing is to follow the guide's pace — going too fast is the primary cause of AMS on this circuit. Trekking insurance covering helicopter evacuation from altitude is not optional for Manaslu; it is a condition of booking with us.

Can I combine the Manaslu Circuit with the Annapurna Circuit?

Yes, and it's a fantastic combination. Finishing at Dharapani places you directly on the Annapurna Circuit. Adding 7–10 days takes you over the Thorong La (5,416m) to Muktinath and Pokhara. The combined trek covers roughly 300km, crosses two major Himalayan passes, and traverses the full ecological and cultural spectrum of central Nepal. Contact our team for the combined itinerary and pricing.

Is the Manaslu Circuit crowded?

By Himalayan trekking standards, no. Even in peak October, daily trekker numbers in the restricted zone are a fraction of those on the Annapurna or Everest circuits. Above Samagaon, you may go a full day without seeing another trekking group. The permit system is, by design, the mechanism that keeps it this way.
