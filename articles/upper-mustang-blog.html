About this trek

Upper Mustang was once the Kingdom of Lo, and Tibetan culture has survived here almost untouched. You walk through eroded canyons, past red-walled monasteries and cliffs full of caves, to Lo Manthang, a walled town of around 150 houses. Because it sits in the rain shadow north of the main Himalaya, it's one of the few treks that works well during the summer monsoon.

  • The walled town and royal palace of Lo Manthang
  • 15th-century wall paintings in Thubchen and Jampa monasteries
  • Sky caves and cliff dwellings around Chhoser
  • Red cliffs of Drakmar and Ghar Gompa
  • Dry trails in July and August, when most of Nepal is wet
  • The Tiji festival in Lo Manthang each May

Permits and practical details

Permits you need

  • Upper Mustang Restricted Area Permit
    Long set at US$500 for 10 days. In November 2025 Nepal's cabinet approved a change to US$50 per person per day
  • Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)
    NPR 3,000 per person

We confirm the fee in force on the day we quote and include it in the price. Permits are issued through a registered agency, with a licensed guide.

Altitude and safety

Above 3,000 m we check everyone's oxygen level and pulse each evening, and talk through how you're sleeping and eating. If altitude symptoms don't ease with rest, we go down. No summit or pass is worth pushing on for. Your insurance needs to cover helicopter rescue up to 4,010 m.

Where you stay

Simple, family-run tea houses: twin rooms, shared bathrooms higher up, and a warm dining room around the stove where everyone gathers in the evening. Hot showers and phone charging cost a few dollars extra.

Fitness and preparation

If you can walk for six hours on hilly ground several days in a row, you'll be fine. Two or three months of regular hill walks with a loaded daypack is the best preparation.

Common questions

Can I trek Upper Mustang in the monsoon?

Yes. Mustang lies north of the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri massifs and gets very little rain, so June to September is a good time to go. Flights to Jomsom can still be delayed by cloud lower down.

When is the Tiji festival?

Tiji is a three-day masked-dance festival in Lo Manthang, held in spring (usually May) on dates set by the Tibetan calendar. We can time your trek to it.

Is there a road now?

Yes, a dirt road runs to Lo Manthang. We walk the old trails, which mostly stay away from it, and use jeeps only where they save a dull day.

How much does the permit cost in 2026?

The flat US$500 for ten days was replaced in late 2025 by a per-day fee of US$50. We quote the fee in force when you book.
