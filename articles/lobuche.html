About this climb

This is the Everest Base Camp trek with a real summit at the end of it. By the time you've been to base camp and up Kala Patthar, you're well acclimatised for Lobuche East: a steeper, more exposed climb than Island Peak, with fixed ropes on snow and a summit ridge that looks straight across at Everest.

  • Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar before the climb
  • The Lobuche East summit ridge, 6,119 m, facing Everest
  • Excellent acclimatisation before summit day
  • Training day at base camp
  • Reserve day for weather
  • One guide to two climbers on summit day

What's included

Included in the price

  • Airport pick-up and drop-off in Kathmandu
  • A licensed climbing guide (1 guide to 2 climbers on summit day) and a trekking guide
  • Porters on the approach, paid and insured
  • Climbing royalty, trekking permits and conservation fees
  • Tea houses on the approach; tents, cook and meals at base and high camp
  • Group climbing gear: fixed rope, anchors, ice screws
  • Skills session at base camp before the summit push
  • Transport and domestic flights shown in the itinerary
  • Satellite communication and a full medical kit with oxygen

Not included

  • International flights and Nepal visa
  • Insurance covering climbing to the summit altitude and helicopter rescue
  • Personal climbing gear (can be hired in Kathmandu or Chhukung)
  • Summit bonus and tips for guides and porters
  • Drinks, showers and charging in tea houses

Permits and practical details

Permits you need

  • Lobuche East climbing permit (Nepal Mountaineering Association)
    Seasonal royalty, lowest in autumn
  • Sagarmatha National Park entry
    NPR 3,000
  • Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality fee
    Collected at Lukla or Monjo

NMA royalties were revised in September 2025. We include the current fee for your season.

Altitude and safety

Above 3,000 m we check everyone's oxygen level and pulse each evening, and talk through how you're sleeping and eating. If altitude symptoms don't ease with rest, we go down. No summit or pass is worth pushing on for. Your insurance needs to cover helicopter rescue up to 6,119 m.

Where you stay

Tea houses on the walk in, then two-person tents at base camp and high camp. At base camp a cook team looks after you, with a dining tent and a toilet tent.

Fitness and preparation

You need good hill fitness, some experience walking in crampons and a head for heights. When you book, we send a training plan and a full kit list, and we check your gear together in Kathmandu.

Common questions

Is Lobuche East harder than Island Peak?

Slightly. The rock slabs to high camp and the exposed final ridge make it more demanding, though the fixed-rope section is similar. Acclimatisation is better because you've already been to 5,644 m on Kala Patthar.

Is the true summit climbed?

Most teams stop at the main summit ridge (6,119 m). The true summit is a short way along a narrow, corniced ridge and is rarely climbed. We will be clear on the day about where we stop.

Can I do just the climb without Everest Base Camp?

Yes, a shorter version goes straight from Dingboche to Lobuche base camp. Ask us for a quote.
