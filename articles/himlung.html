About this climb

Himlung Himal (7,126 m) rises on the Tibetan border north of the Annapurnas. It's a glaciated, mostly non-technical climb and one of the best first 7,000 m peaks, a real step up in altitude without the cost and difficulty of an 8,000 m mountain. The walk in, through the medieval villages of Nar Phu, is an adventure in itself.

  • A 7,000 m summit on the Tibetan border
  • The restricted Nar Phu valley and the stone village of Phu
  • Mostly non-technical glacier climbing
  • Good preparation for an 8,000 m peak
  • Rotations and rest days on a proper expedition schedule
  • Small team with personal climbing Sherpa on summit day

What's included

Included in the price

  • Airport pick-up and drop-off in Kathmandu
  • A licensed climbing guide (1 guide to 2 climbers on summit day) and a trekking guide
  • Porters on the approach, paid and insured
  • Climbing royalty, trekking permits and conservation fees
  • Tea houses on the approach; tents, cook and meals at base and high camp
  • Group climbing gear: fixed rope, anchors, ice screws
  • Skills session at base camp before the summit push
  • Transport and domestic flights shown in the itinerary
  • Satellite communication and a full medical kit with oxygen

Not included

  • International flights and Nepal visa
  • Insurance covering climbing to the summit altitude and helicopter rescue
  • Personal climbing gear (can be hired in Kathmandu or Chhukung)
  • Summit bonus and tips for guides and porters
  • Drinks, showers and charging in tea houses

Permits and practical details

Permits you need

  • Himlung Himal climbing royalty (Department of Tourism)
    Seasonal; revised in September 2025
  • Nar Phu Restricted Area Permit
    Weekly fee, higher in autumn
  • Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)
    NPR 3,000

All royalties and permits are included in the price.

Altitude and safety

Above 3,000 m we check everyone's oxygen level and pulse each evening, and talk through how you're sleeping and eating. If altitude symptoms don't ease with rest, we go down. No summit or pass is worth pushing on for. Your insurance needs to cover helicopter rescue up to 7,126 m.

Where you stay

Tea houses on the walk in, then two-person tents at base camp and high camp. At base camp a cook team looks after you, with a dining tent and a toilet tent.

Fitness and preparation

You need good hill fitness, some experience walking in crampons and a head for heights. When you book, we send a training plan and a full kit list, and we check your gear together in Kathmandu.

Common questions

Is Himlung a good first 7,000 m peak?

Yes. The route is mostly a glacier and snow climb on fixed lines, with a few steeper sections, and the approach gives good acclimatisation. You should have climbed at least one 6,000 m peak first.

Will I use supplemental oxygen?

Most climbers summit without it. Oxygen is carried for emergencies and can be added for summit day on request.

Why is Nar Phu restricted?

It lies close to the Tibetan border and was closed to foreigners until 2003. Permits are issued through a registered agency to groups with a licensed guide.
