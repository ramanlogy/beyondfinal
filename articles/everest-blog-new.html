The Everest Base Camp Trek is not just a walk — it's a pilgrimage into the heart of the world's most dramatic mountain landscape. Every year, tens of thousands of trekkers travel to Nepal to make this iconic journey through the Khumbu region, following the same trails used by legendary mountaineers including Sir Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay.

This guide, updated for 2026, draws on the experience of Beyond Summit's guides who have collectively completed this route over 400 times. Whether you're a seasoned trekker or setting foot on a Himalayan trail for the first time, here is everything you need to know.

What is the EBC Trek?

Everest Base Camp sits at 5,364 metres (17,598 feet) above sea level, at the foot of the Khumbu Icefall on the Nepalese side of Mount Everest. The classic trek starts and ends in Lukla, reached by a short mountain flight from Kathmandu, and covers approximately 130 kilometres of trail through Sagarmatha National Park — a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The route climbs through Sherpa villages, ancient Buddhist monasteries, rhododendron forests (in season), and the glaciated upper Khumbu, before arriving at the largest high-altitude base camp in the world. It's a journey through living Himalayan culture as much as it is a physical challenge.

Key Facts at a Glance

Distance: ~130km total (Lukla–Lukla)  ·  Maximum altitude: 5,364m (EBC) / 5,550m (Kala Patthar)  ·  Standard duration: 12–16 days  ·  Best seasons: March–May and October–November  ·  Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous  ·  Technical climbing: None required

Best Time to Trek

The Khumbu Valley is trekkable year-round, but two seasons dominate the EBC trekking calendar.

Spring — March to May

Spring is the most popular trekking season, coinciding with the Everest climbing season. April and May offer the most stable weather, comfortable daytime temperatures (5–12°C at altitude), and spectacular views before the monsoon haze arrives. The trails are busiest during this period — Namche Bazaar in May can feel surprisingly lively — but for a reason: the conditions are exceptional.

Autumn — October to November

Autumn is our personal favourite at Beyond Summit. Post-monsoon skies are crystal clear, offering exceptional photographic visibility. Temperatures are slightly cooler than spring, and trails are less crowded. October offers the finest balance of weather and views.

Monsoon (June–September)

While technically passable, monsoon season brings persistent cloud cover, leeches, flooded trails, and flight delays. Most experienced trekkers and operators avoid this window. December through February is cold — very cold above 4,000m — but offers dramatic winter solitude for experienced high-altitude trekkers.

14-Day Itinerary

Below is Beyond Summit's standard 14-day EBC itinerary. We incorporate three acclimatization days rather than the two used by budget operators — a significant safety difference at this altitude.

Day1
Kathmandu → Lukla → Phakding
An early morning flight brings you to Lukla (2,860m) — one of the most thrilling runways in the world. A 3–4 hour walk along the Dudh Koshi River leads to Phakding (2,610m).

Day2
Phakding → Namche Bazaar
The biggest single ascent of the trek — 800 vertical metres through forest and over suspension bridges to reach the Sherpa capital, Namche Bazaar (3,440m). First views of Everest on a clear day.
~11 km+830 m altitude
Day3
Acclimatization Day — Namche Bazaar
A mandatory rest day. We recommend a day hike up to the Everest View Hotel (3,962m) for stunning panoramic views, returning to sleep at 3,440m. Visit Namche's Saturday market if timing aligns.

Day4
Namche → Tengboche
Pass through Kyangjuma with breathtaking views of Ama Dablam, Everest, and Lhotse. Tengboche Monastery (3,867m) is the spiritual heart of the Khumbu — arrive in time for the evening puja ceremony.
~10 km+427 m altitude
Day5
Tengboche → Dingboche
Descend to Deboche, cross the Imja Khola, and climb through rhododendron forest to Pangboche before continuing to Dingboche (4,410m) — the gateway to the high Khumbu.

Second acclimatization day. Hike to Nagarjun Hill (5,083m) for phenomenal views of Island Peak, Makalu, and Lhotse South Face. This altitude day hike is excellent preparation for what lies ahead.

The culminating day. Reach Gorak Shep (5,140m), drop your pack, and push on to Everest Base Camp (5,364m). Stand on the Khumbu Glacier and look up at the route taken by every Everest climber. Return to Gorak Shep.

Pro Tip — Acclimatization

Follow the golden rule: "Climb high, sleep low." Beyond Summit adds a third acclimatization day above 4,000m compared to budget operators. This dramatically reduces the risk of Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS). If you feel unwell at altitude — persistent headache, nausea, loss of coordination — descent is the only cure. Do not ascend with symptoms.

Permits Required 2026

All trekkers need two permits to trek in the Khumbu region:

  1. Sagarmatha National Park Permit — NPR 3,000 (~USD 22) per person. Encompasses the entire Khumbu Valley including Everest.
  2. TIMS Card (Trekkers' Information Management System) — NPR 2,000 (~USD 15) for independent trekkers; covered by your agent if booking with a licensed operator like Beyond Summit.

When you trek with Beyond Summit Expeditions, all permits are arranged on your behalf before departure and included in the package cost. Since April 2023, solo unguided trekking in Nepal's national parks is no longer legally permitted — all trekkers must be accompanied by a licensed guide.

Fitness Requirements

The EBC trek requires no mountaineering skills, but it does demand a solid base of fitness. You will be walking for 5–8 hours per day at altitudes above 3,000 metres, often on uneven terrain. The cumulative elevation gain over the trek exceeds 8,000 metres.

We recommend a minimum of 8–12 weeks of dedicated preparation before your trek, including:

  • Weekly long hikes (3–6 hours) on hilly terrain, ideally with a loaded daypack
  • Cardiovascular training 3–4 times per week (running, cycling, swimming)
  • Stair climbs with a weighted pack to simulate ascent effort
  • Core and leg strengthening exercises (squats, lunges, step-ups)

If you are over 50 or have any pre-existing cardiovascular or respiratory conditions, consult your physician and consider a high-altitude medical consultation before booking.

Essential Gear Checklist

Packing correctly is half the battle. Your base weight should not exceed 10–12kg; porters (included in Beyond Summit packages) carry the bulk of your kit.

  • Footwear: Waterproof, ankle-supporting trekking boots (broken in before the trek), warm camp shoes/sandals, gaiters
  • Layering system: Moisture-wicking base layers, mid-layer fleece or softshell, insulated down jacket (800+ fill), waterproof hardshell jacket and trousers
  • Sleeping: Down sleeping bag rated to –15°C (most teahouse rooms lack adequate heating above 4,500m)
  • Accessories: Warm hat, balaclava, liner gloves + waterproof outer gloves, UV-protective sunglasses, SPF 50+ sunscreen
  • Trekking poles: Strongly recommended, especially for descent
  • Medications: Ibuprofen, diamox (consult GP), oral rehydration salts, blister kit, personal prescriptions
  • Electronics: Power bank (teahouses charge for electricity), camera, offline maps downloaded

Everything you need — sleeping bags, down jackets, trekking poles, duffel bags — can be rented affordably in Kathmandu's Thamel district. Beyond Summit arranges quality gear rental as part of your pre-departure briefing day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a guide for the EBC trek?

Since April 2023, solo, unguided trekking in Nepal's national parks is no longer legally permitted. All trekkers must be accompanied by a licensed guide. Beyond Summit's guides are TIMS-registered, fluent in English, and trained in wilderness first aid.

Can I trek EBC without prior high-altitude experience?

Yes. The EBC trek reaches 5,364m, which is challenging but manageable without prior Himalayan experience if you are fit, follow your guide's pace, and take acclimatization seriously. We have guided trekkers in their 60s and 70s to Base Camp.

What is the failure rate on the EBC trek?

Industry statistics suggest 7–10% of trekkers do not reach Base Camp, primarily due to altitude sickness. Beyond Summit's itinerary — with three acclimatization days — gives our guests a significantly higher completion rate. Turnaround decisions are always made by our guides in consultation with clients, and never rushed.
