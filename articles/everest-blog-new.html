About this trek

This is the classic walk to the foot of Everest. You follow the Dudh Koshi river up through Sherpa villages to Namche Bazaar, past the monastery at Tengboche and onto the moraine of the Khumbu Glacier. We build in two rest days on the way up, and on day nine you climb Kala Patthar at sunrise for the finest view of Everest's summit you can get without climbing it.

  • Sunrise on Everest from Kala Patthar, 5,644 m
  • Standing at Everest Base Camp beside the Khumbu Icefall
  • Namche Bazaar, the Sherpa trading town
  • Evening prayers at Tengboche Monastery
  • Ama Dablam in view for most of the walk
  • Two acclimatisation days built in

    Day 1: Fly to Lukla, walk to Phakding

    about 8 km3 to 4 hSleep 2,610 m

    Day 2: Phakding to Namche Bazaar

    about 10 km6 to 7 hSleep 3,440 m

    Into Sagarmatha National Park at Monjo, then across the high Hillary Suspension Bridge and a long climb to Namche. On a clear day you get a first look at Everest halfway up the hill.

    Tea house, twin roomMeals: B, L, D
  3. Day3

    Day 3: Rest day in NamcheAcclimatisation

    about 5 km3 to 4 hHigh point 3,880 mSleep 3,440 m

    Tea house, twin roomMeals: B, L, D
  4. Day4

    Day 4: Namche to Tengboche

    about 10 km5 to 6 hSleep 3,867 m

    A balcony trail with Ama Dablam ahead, a drop to the river at Phunki Tenga and a steady climb through rhododendron forest to Tengboche Monastery in time for afternoon prayers.

    Tea house, twin roomMeals: B, L, D
  5. Day5

    Day 5: Tengboche to Dingboche

    about 11 km5 to 6 hSleep 4,410 m

    Past the old gompa at Pangboche and above the tree line into the Imja valley. Dingboche is a village of stone-walled potato fields below Lhotse and Island Peak.

    Day 6: Rest day in DingbocheAcclimatisation

    about 5 km4 to 5 hHigh point 5,083 mSleep 4,410 m

    Day 7: Dingboche to Lobuche

    about 8 km5 hSleep 4,940 m

    Up the open Pheriche valley, then a steep pull onto the Khumbu moraine at Thukla, where stone memorials remember climbers who died on Everest.

Permits and practical details

Permits you need

  • Sagarmatha National Park entry
    NPR 3,000 per person (foreign nationals)
  • Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality fee
    Collected at Lukla or Monjo

A TIMS card is not used in the Khumbu. Both fees are included in our price.

Altitude and safety

Above 3,000 m we check everyone's oxygen level and pulse each evening, and talk through how you're sleeping and eating. If altitude symptoms don't ease with rest, we go down. No summit or pass is worth pushing on for. Your insurance needs to cover helicopter rescue up to 5,644 m.

Where you stay

Simple, family-run tea houses: twin rooms, shared bathrooms higher up, and a warm dining room around the stove where everyone gathers in the evening. Hot showers and phone charging cost a few dollars extra.

Fitness and preparation

If you can walk for six hours on hilly ground several days in a row, you'll be fine. Two or three months of regular hill walks with a loaded daypack is the best preparation.

Common questions

How hard is the Everest Base Camp trek?

There is no climbing, but you walk five to seven hours a day for twelve days and sleep above 4,000 m for six nights. Anyone who can hike a full day in hills at home and trains for two or three months beforehand can do it. Altitude, not fitness, is what stops most people, which is why we build in two rest days.

Do Lukla flights really leave from Ramechhap?

In the busy months (roughly April to May and October to November) Nepal's aviation authority routes most Kathmandu to Lukla flights through Manthali airport in Ramechhap to ease congestion. That means a 4 to 5 hour drive before dawn. We arrange the transport and keep a spare day at the end for weather delays.

Do I need a guide?

Since 2023 Nepal requires foreign trekkers in national parks to walk with a licensed guide from a registered agency. All our treks include one.

What insurance do I need?

A policy that covers trekking up to 6,000 m and helicopter evacuation. Rescue from Gorak Shep by helicopter costs several thousand US dollars without it.

Can I charge my phone and use Wi-Fi?

Yes, most tea houses sell charging and Wi-Fi by the hour, and many villages have mobile signal. Prices rise with altitude, so bring a power bank.
