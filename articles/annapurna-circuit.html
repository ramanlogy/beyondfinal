About this trek

The Annapurna Circuit is Nepal's great loop around the Annapurna massif. You start among rice terraces, follow the Marsyangdi valley up to the dry, Tibetan-style villages around Manang, detour to Tilicho Lake and cross the Thorong La at 5,416 m to the temple town of Muktinath. A road now covers parts of the old route, so we drive those stretches and walk the quieter high trails instead.

  • Crossing the Thorong La, 5,416 m
  • Tilicho Lake, one of the highest lakes of its size in the world
  • The upper route through Ngawal, away from the road
  • Muktinath, sacred to Hindus and Buddhists
  • The windswept Kali Gandaki and Kagbeni
  • Hot springs at Tatopani and sunrise from Poon Hill

Permits and practical details

Permits you need

  • Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)
    NPR 3,000 per person (foreign nationals)
  • TIMS card
    Required in the Annapurna region

Both are included in our price.

Altitude and safety

Above 3,000 m we check everyone's oxygen level and pulse each evening, and talk through how you're sleeping and eating. If altitude symptoms don't ease with rest, we go down. No summit or pass is worth pushing on for. Your insurance needs to cover helicopter rescue up to 5,416 m.

Where you stay

Simple, family-run tea houses: twin rooms, shared bathrooms higher up, and a warm dining room around the stove where everyone gathers in the evening. Hot showers and phone charging cost a few dollars extra.

Fitness and preparation

If you can walk for six hours on hilly ground several days in a row, you'll be fine. Two or three months of regular hill walks with a loaded daypack is the best preparation.

Common questions

Is the Annapurna Circuit ruined by the road?

A dirt road now runs up both valleys, but much of it can be avoided. We drive the busy lower sections and walk the high alternative trails (Ngawal, Tilicho, Kagbeni), where you will rarely see a vehicle.

How hard is the Thorong La?

It is the longest day of the trek: around four hours of climbing in thin air before dawn, then a steep 1,600 m descent. It is a walk, not a climb, but snow can close it for a day or two in winter and early spring.

Can I skip Tilicho Lake?

Yes. Leaving out Tilicho takes three days off the trip. We would add a second rest day in Manang instead.

When is the best time to go?

October and November give the clearest skies. March and April are also good, with more snow on the pass early in the season.
