About this climb

Island Peak (Imja Tse) is the most popular first Himalayan summit. After a week of trekking through the Khumbu you climb a rocky ridge, cross a glacier and go up a steep headwall on fixed ropes to a narrow summit ridge, with the south face of Lhotse towering above. You need fitness and a day of rope training, not previous expedition experience.

  • A 6,189 m summit with Lhotse's south face behind
  • Training day on ropes, jumar and crampons at base camp
  • Chhukung Ri (5,550 m) as an acclimatisation climb
  • The full Khumbu trek via Namche and Tengboche
  • Reserve summit day for bad weather
  • One guide to two climbers on summit day

Permits and practical details

Permits you need

  • Island Peak climbing permit (Nepal Mountaineering Association)
    Seasonal royalty, lowest in autumn
  • Sagarmatha National Park entry
    NPR 3,000
  • Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality fee
    Collected at Lukla or Monjo

NMA royalties were revised in September 2025. We include the current fee for your season in the price.

Altitude and safety

Above 3,000 m we check everyone's oxygen level and pulse each evening, and talk through how you're sleeping and eating. If altitude symptoms don't ease with rest, we go down. No summit or pass is worth pushing on for. Your insurance needs to cover helicopter rescue up to 6,189 m.

Where you stay

Tea houses on the walk in, then two-person tents at base camp and high camp. At base camp a cook team looks after you, with a dining tent and a toilet tent.

Fitness and preparation

You need good hill fitness, some experience walking in crampons and a head for heights. When you book, we send a training plan and a full kit list, and we check your gear together in Kathmandu.

Common questions

Do I need climbing experience for Island Peak?

You don't need previous Himalayan climbs, but you should be a strong hill walker and comfortable with exposure. A winter skills or crampon course at home helps a lot. We teach fixed-rope technique at base camp.

How technical is the headwall?

It is about 100 m of 40 to 45 degree snow and ice on fixed ropes, followed by a narrow ridge to the summit. In late autumn the glacier crevasses can be wider and need ladders.

What gear do I need?

Double or insulated mountaineering boots, crampons, harness, helmet, jumar, figure-of-eight, slings and karabiners, plus down jacket and warm gloves. Everything can be hired in Kathmandu or Chhukung; we send a full list.

What is the success rate?

It depends on the weather and on how well each climber acclimatises. Our itinerary gives you two acclimatisation days, a climb of Chhukung Ri and a reserve day, which gives you the best chance.
